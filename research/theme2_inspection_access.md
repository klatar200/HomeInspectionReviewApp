## How to read this file
- Evidence strength: STRONG = statute text, regulator finding, builder's own published policy, or major news. MEDIUM = multiple consistent secondary sources. WEAK = single anecdote.
- Source status: FETCHED = page content retrieved and read this session. SEEN-IN-RESULTS = appeared in search results only (content known only via search-result snippet/summary; not independently read).
- Quotes are verbatim from fetched text unless marked "(snippet)". Quotes are <=25 words.
- Forum posts are anecdotes by nature. Where a "STRONG" label would be wrong because the underlying source is a single inspector's account reported by a trade outlet, I have downgraded it.

## 1. Builders prohibiting or limiting third-party inspections (stage / timing / who may enter)

### 1.1 Pre-drywall / pre-pour / framing inspections refused; final-walkthrough-only
- Rule/practice: Sales agent says builder does not allow a hired inspector at pre-drywall walkthrough, citing "liability" and the city inspection. Another poster: inspector allowed "for the final walk-through only."
- Reported by: forum posters (buyers). Builder: unnamed (multiple). Jurisdiction: not stated (Irvine-area forum). Date: May 11-12, 2014.
- Source type: forum/anecdote. URL: https://www.talkirvine.com/threads/builder-doesnt-allow-3rd-party-inspector-for-pre-drywall-walkthrough.11702 (FETCHED).
- Quote: "will allow an inspector for the final walk-through only"
- Strength: WEAK (two anecdotes in one thread). Counter-evidence in same thread: a poster says KB Homes allowed a third-party inspector pre-drywall with "unlimited access" and fixed most issues. One poster speculates motive: "I think the main reason they won't allow pre-drywall inspection is it can delay their schedule." (opinion, not evidence of motive).

### 1.2 Maronda Homes: no third-party pre-drywall; third-party final only after buyer orientation
- Rule/practice: Alleged that Maronda stopped allowing third-party pre-drywall inspections; final third-party inspections only after the orientation walkthrough and sign-off.
- Reported by: one inspector (pmcmanus) on InterNACHI forum, Dec 2, 2025. Builder: Maronda Homes (location not stated). (The tool's summary did not establish Maronda's parent company; not asserted here.)
- Source type: forum/anecdote. URL: https://forum.nachi.org/t/d-r-horton-at-it-again/262591 (FETCHED).
- Quote: "Maronda homes no longer allows clients to have a predrywall inspection by a third party inspector."
- Strength: WEAK. Same thread: another poster (mwilles): "Many home builders have written into the purchase agreement they will only allow a pre-closing inspection." (WEAK, general claim).

### 1.3 Contract clauses / "hurdles just short of banning"
- Rule/practice: Inspectors in multiple regions say builders create hurdles "just short of out-right banning inspections"; one small builder refused both pre-drywall and pre-closing inspections; inspector later found a 1.5-inch stair riser variation after closing. Virginia poster: signed buyer inspection waiver makes inspection "a no-go"; post-closing rejection period (3-14 days) is the workaround.
- Reported by: inspectors (Lon Henderson, Martin Willes, Mark Masengale). Builder: "Richmond" named for Henderson's area (ambiguous; may be Richmond American or the city; not clarified), others unnamed. Jurisdiction: Virginia/Colorado area per posts. Date: May 16-17, 2022.
- Source type: forum/anecdote. URL: https://forum.nachi.org/t/are-builders-allowed-to-deny-3rd-party-inspections/215727?page=2 (FETCHED).
- Quote (fragment): "just short of out-right banning inspections"
- Strength: MEDIUM (several consistent inspector accounts across threads 2004-2026, but no primary document).

### 1.4 Outright denial / ban on site (older)
- Rule/practice: Tract/spec builder "forbid a HI from coming on site"; reportedly written into client contracts. Mid-2000s: Phoenix-area and San Antonio accounts of release/indemnity demands (see 3.1).
- Builder: unnamed. Date: July 2007 (and 2004 for San Antonio). Source type: forum/anecdote. URL: https://forum.nachi.org/t/builder-dislikes-home-inspections/18438 (FETCHED).
- Quote: none <=25 words verbatim beyond the paraphrase above; poster Doug Edwards says truly good builders "have never been offended or put off by me being there."
- Strength: WEAK (old, single poster).

### 1.5 Builder publishes that outside inspections ARE allowed (Lennar) - with conditions
- Rule/practice: Lennar's own page lets buyers have the home inspected by a professional of their choice before closing, but: coordinated through the New Home Consultant/Construction Manager; buyer pays; must be completed "a couple of days" before orientation; report shared at least two days before orientation; appliances may not yet be installed.

### 1.6 What the Nevada buyer guide found about published builder policies
- Rule/practice: A Las Vegas realtor guide (published May 11, 2026, updated Oct 4, 2026) states none of five builders (Lennar, Pulte, KB Home, Toll Brothers, Richmond American) publishes an explicit rule barring a buyer's inspector at pre-drywall. - Quote: "often require two to four weeks notice" (attributed to First Home Inspectors, per the article).
- Strength: WEAK-MEDIUM for the specific requirements (vendors, not builder documents). The negative finding (no builder publishes a pre-drywall ban) is useful: most restrictions live in contracts/access agreements/oral sales-office statements and are NOT published.

## 2. Operational limits: escort, roof, attic, ladders, appliances/HVAC, destructive testing, photos/video

## 10. Cross-cutting notes for product design (not legal advice)
- Builder owns the site until closing; multiple posters say builders can lawfully set access conditions absent a contract right (opinion, not verified legal analysis). The decisive document is the purchase agreement, which is rarely published.
- Published primary evidence is thin: Lennar's page (1.5) is the only major-builder policy fetched. Most "rules" reach the public via inspectors, so the platform should collect uploaded contract excerpts/access agreements as evidence (consistent with D-008) rather than rely on hearsay.
- Many items are stale (2004-2014). AS-OF must be shown and >90 days flagged for re-verification (project convention).
- Allegations vs findings: only the ROC Lennar matter (6.1), BTR Porter outcome (5.1), NJ SCI (7.1) and statutes are regulator/government outputs. Everything else is allegation.
- Counter-evidence to keep: KB Homes allowed pre-drywall access in a 2014 buyer anecdote; Lennar publishes permission; many builders reportedly accept inspectors (several forum posters, 2022-2025); some builders offer to pay for inspections.
